Most "best to-do app" lists rank apps by feature count. That's the wrong metric for ADHD brains
— more features usually means more setup decisions, and setup decisions are exactly where ADHD
task apps go to die in the app graveyard on your phone's third home screen.

This guide sorts apps by **what kind of ADHD brain they're actually built for**, not by how many
integrations they have.

> vetting checklist). Nothing here is ranked by commission — it's ranked by fit.

## If your problem is "I forget tasks exist": Todoist

Todoist's strength is capture speed — natural-language quick-add ("email Sam tomorrow 3pm")
turns a fleeting thought into a stored task in seconds, before it evaporates. That speed matters
more for ADHD brains than almost any other feature, because the gap between "I should do that"
and "I forgot I thought that" can be measured in seconds.

**Good fit if:** your main issue is tasks disappearing from memory before you can act on them.
**Less good fit if:** you need visual, time-blocked structure rather than a list.

## If your problem is "I have a list but no sense of *when*": Structured / Tiimo

Structured and Tiimo both replace the list format with a visual timeline of the day. For a brain
that struggles with time-blindness (a well-documented ADHD trait — internal time tracking is
unreliable, not a discipline issue), seeing the day as blocks on a timeline does something a list
can't: it makes "when" as concrete as "what."

**Good fit if:** you finish your to-do list at 11pm having done things in the wrong order all
day. **Less good fit if:** your day is too unpredictable for time-blocking to survive contact
with reality.

## If your problem is "everything lives in fifteen different apps": Notion

Notion trades speed for flexibility — it can hold your tasks, notes, and trackers in one place,
which matters if scattered systems are part of why things get missed. The tradeoff is real
setup time up front, which can itself become a procrastination trap ("I'll be productive once
I've built the perfect Notion system").

**Good fit if:** you've already tried several single-purpose apps and the *switching* between
them was the actual problem. **Less good fit if:** you know from experience that open-ended
tools become a hyperfocus project instead of a task manager.

## If your problem is "I do better with someone else watching": body doubling apps

Task apps that add a body-doubling feature (a shared virtual space where you work alongside
others, cameras optional) address something none of the apps above solve: task initiation.
Knowing *what* to do isn't the same problem as being able to *start* doing it — body doubling
targets the second one directly, and it's one of the better-evidenced informal ADHD strategies
for that specific gap.

## How to actually pick one

Don't trial three apps in the same week — that just adds a fourth task ("evaluate apps") on top
of the pile. Pick based on which failure mode below sounds like your week:

- Forget tasks exist → **Todoist**
- Know the tasks, lose track of time → **Structured or Tiimo**
- Tasks are scattered across too many places → **Notion**
- Know what to do, can't start → a **body doubling** tool alongside whichever list app you keep
