single biggest reason people buy one, try it twice, and shove it in a closet.

## Why weighted blankets work (the honest version)

The working theory is deep pressure stimulation — even, distributed pressure across the body that
can have a calming effect on the nervous system for some people, similar to the effect of a firm
hug or a swaddle. It's not universal, and it's not a treatment for ADHD itself — it's a sensory
tool some people find helps with settling down, especially at night or during high-anxiety
moments.

## The one spec that matters most: weight ratio

The standard guidance is **roughly 10% of your body weight**, though some people prefer slightly
heavier or lighter — this is genuinely personal and worth treating as a starting point, not a
rule. A blanket that's too light won't produce a noticeable effect; one that's too heavy becomes
uncomfortable to move under, which defeats the purpose entirely.

## Material matters more than most guides admit

- **Glass bead fill** — the most common weighting method, distributes weight evenly, tends to be
  quieter than plastic pellet fill
- **Cotton or bamboo cover** — better for anyone who sleeps hot, which matters a lot given how
  much heat a weighted blanket traps by design
- **Minky or fleece cover** — softer texture, but retains more heat — a poor pick for summer use
  or for anyone who already runs warm at night

## The mistake almost everyone makes

Buying one blanket for year-round use. A weighted blanket that's comfortable in January is often
unusable in July because of heat retention, not weight. If budget allows, a breathable
cotton-covered option for summer and a heavier, warmer one for winter solves this — otherwise,
default to the more breathable material, since "too warm" is the more common complaint than
"not warm enough."

## Where a weighted blanket fits into a broader system

A weighted blanket helps with settling down, not with the routine that gets you there in the
first place — pair it with a consistent wind-down structure like
[ADHD Night Routine Checklist](/blog/adhd-night-routine-checklist) rather than expecting the
blanket alone to fix a chaotic bedtime.
