Body doubling is the single most-recommended informal ADHD strategy in online communities, and
also one of the most misunderstood. It's not about accountability, and it's not about someone
checking your work. It's about presence.

## What body doubling actually is

Body doubling means working alongside another person — in the same room, on a video call, or in
a virtual co-working space — while each of you does your own, unrelated task. Neither of you is
supervising the other. The other person doesn't even need to know what you're working on.

The mechanism isn't fully settled science, but the working theory in ADHD communities is that
having another person present lowers the activation energy needed to *start* a task, which is
where ADHD executive dysfunction tends to bite hardest — not in knowing what to do, but in
crossing the gap between knowing and doing.

## Free ways to try it before paying for anything

- **A video call with a friend, cameras on, no talking required.** This costs nothing and tests
  whether body doubling works for you at all before you commit to a subscription.
- **Public library or coffee shop.** The presence of strangers can produce a similar effect for
  some people — it's worth testing since it requires no app and no scheduling.
- **Free tiers of co-working apps.** Several body doubling apps offer a limited number of free
  sessions per week — enough to know if the format clicks before upgrading.

## What to look for in a paid app

If the free version works for you, here's what actually matters when comparing paid options —
in order of importance:

1. **Scheduled sessions vs. drop-in anytime.** Some people need a fixed appointment to show up
   for; others need the option to start a session the moment they notice they're stuck. Know
   which one you are before picking a plan.
2. **Camera-optional rooms.** Requiring video for everyone filters out people who'd otherwise
   use the tool — look for apps that make video opt-in.
3. **Session length.** 25–50 minute blocks tend to match attention spans better than open-ended
   rooms with no natural stopping point.
4. **Community size at your usual working hours.** A body doubling app with plenty of members at
   2pm and nobody at 11pm (or vice versa) won't help if that's when you actually work.

## The honest limitation

Body doubling helps with *starting* a task and staying in the room while doing it. It does very
little for planning what the task even is — pair it with a task list (see
[Best To-Do List Apps for ADHD Adults in 2026](/blog/best-to-do-list-apps-for-adhd-2026)) rather
than expecting it to replace one.
