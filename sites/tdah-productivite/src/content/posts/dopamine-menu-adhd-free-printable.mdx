A dopamine menu is a pre-made, categorized list of activities you can turn to when you're
understimulated, overwhelmed, or stuck — sorted the way a restaurant menu is sorted, so you don't
have to invent an answer to "what do I need right now" while you're already low on the executive
function needed to answer that question.

## Why this works better than deciding in the moment

When you're dysregulated — bored, overstimulated, avoidant, or just stuck — is exactly the worst
time to generate a good idea from scratch. A dopamine menu moves that decision to a calmer moment
in advance, so in the hard moment you're just *choosing from a list*, which is a much smaller ask
than *coming up with an idea*.

## The four categories

Most dopamine menus are organized like an actual restaurant menu:

- **Starters** — quick, low-commitment options for when you have 2-5 minutes (stretch, step
  outside, splash cold water on your face, one song)
- **Mains** — the substantial options for when you have real time and need real engagement
  (a hobby session, a walk, calling a friend, a proper workout)
- **Sides** — things that pair well alongside another task, for when you're doing something
  boring and need a bit of extra stimulation to get through it (music, a fidget tool, a snack)
- **Desserts** — things you enjoy but that are easy to overdo, worth having named honestly so you
  can choose them consciously instead of falling into them by default (social media, a show,
  a game)

## Building your own

The categories above are a starting structure, not a fixed rulebook — a dopamine menu only works
if the items on it are things that are actually true for you. A generic list written by someone
else is a decent starting point, but the version that actually gets used is the one you've edited
to match what genuinely works for your own brain.

A few prompts to fill it in:

- What's something under 5 minutes that reliably shifts your mood?
- What's a "main" you keep meaning to do more but forget is an option in the moment?
- What's a "dessert" you'd rather choose on purpose than fall into by default?
