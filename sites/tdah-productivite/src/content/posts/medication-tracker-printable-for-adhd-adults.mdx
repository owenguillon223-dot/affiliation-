This isn't medical advice, and it isn't a substitute for your prescriber's instructions — it's a
simple logging tool for a specific, common problem: showing up to a doctor's appointment and not
being able to answer "how has it been working" with anything more specific than "fine, I think."

## Why memory alone doesn't work here

ADHD affects the same memory and time-tracking systems that would otherwise let you reliably
recall "I took it at 8am most days, missed it twice, and the afternoon slump started around
week two" — which is exactly the kind of detail a prescriber actually needs to adjust a dose or
timing. Trying to reconstruct that from memory in the waiting room tends to produce vague answers
that don't give your doctor much to work with.

## What's actually useful to track

Keep this simple — a tracker that takes two minutes a day gets used; one that takes ten doesn't.

- **Date and time taken** (or a note if skipped, and why if you remember)
- **Any side effects noticed**, even minor ones — patterns matter more than single incidents
- **A single word or short phrase for how the day went** — "focused," "flat," "irritable,"
  "crashed by 3pm" — specific enough to be useful, short enough to actually write down

## What to leave out

Resist the urge to build an elaborate tracking system with a dozen metrics — the goal is a
record you'll actually maintain for weeks, not a perfect dataset you abandon after four days.
A tracker that's too detailed to keep up with is worse than a simple one you finish.

## Bringing it to your appointment

A few weeks of even rough notes gives your prescriber something concrete to work with instead of
your best guess in the moment — patterns across days matter more than any single day's entry, so
don't worry about a day or two of missing data breaking the usefulness of the log.

## Keep it visible, same as everything else

A printed tracker kept near where you take your medication — not in an app you'd have to
remember to open — is more likely to actually get filled in. The same visibility principle covered
in [ADHD Morning Routine for People Who Hate Mornings](/blog/adhd-morning-routine-for-people-who-hate-mornings)
applies directly here.
