A wedding dress is one of the few purchases most people make once, wear for a matter of hours,
and then store indefinitely. For a micro wedding or elopement — where formality expectations are
often already lower — renting is a genuinely practical option that traditional bridal advice
tends to underrate.

## Why renting fits this format especially well

Elopements and micro weddings are more likely to involve travel, outdoor settings, or physical
activity (a hike to a ceremony spot) than a traditional ballroom wedding — conditions where a
rented dress that doesn't need to survive for decades as a keepsake makes practical sense in a
way it might not for a large formal wedding.

## What to check before renting

- **Return timeline and condition requirements** — understand exactly what counts as normal wear
  versus damage before the dress arrives, since policies vary significantly between rental
  services
- **Alteration policy** — some rental services allow basic alterations (hemming), others don't;
  confirm before assuming you can adjust fit
- **Backup plan for shipping delays** — order with enough buffer time before your date that a
  shipping delay doesn't become a crisis, especially if travel to an elopement location is
  involved

## When buying still makes more sense

- If preserving the dress as a keepsake matters to you personally
- If your body size or the local climate makes rental sizing or timelines risky
- If you want the flexibility to alter significantly or wear it again for another occasion

## A middle option: secondhand

Buying secondhand (resale marketplaces, sample sales) often lands at a similar price point to
renting while still letting you keep the dress — worth considering if the "not owning it"
tradeoff of renting doesn't sit right with you.

## For suits and other formal attire

The same rental logic applies to suits and formalwear for anyone in the wedding party — rental
tends to be underused for elopements and micro weddings specifically, even though the format's
lower overall costs make the savings proportionally more noticeable.
