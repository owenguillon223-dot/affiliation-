Wedding budget breakdowns you'll find online are almost always built for a 100+ guest wedding,
which makes them close to useless for a 20-40 guest micro wedding — the categories that dominate
a traditional budget (catering, venue capacity, rentals for a large space) shrink dramatically,
while a few categories barely change at all.

> Note: the ranges below are general planning estimates to help you allocate a budget, not
> sourced pricing data — actual costs vary widely by region, season, and vendor. Treat this as a
> starting framework, not a quote.

## What shrinks the most with a smaller guest count

- **Catering** — priced per head in almost every case, so this is where guest-count reduction
  saves the most in absolute terms
- **Venue rental** — smaller spaces (a garden, a small private room, a backyard) cost meaningfully
  less than banquet-hall-scale venues built for large events
- **Rentals** (tables, chairs, linens) — scale directly with guest count

## What barely changes regardless of guest count

- **Photography and videography** — most photographers charge by time or package, not by guest
  count, so this is often a larger percentage of a micro wedding budget than a traditional one
- **Your outfit(s)** — dress, suit, alterations don't change with guest count
- **Officiant fees** — typically flat, not per-guest
- **Marriage license and legal fees** — fixed regardless of wedding size

## A rough allocation framework

Rather than fixed dollar amounts (which vary too much by region to be useful), think in
percentages relative to your specific total budget:

- Venue + catering: still often the largest combined category, even shrunk
- Photography: proportionally larger than in a traditional-size wedding budget
- Attire: proportionally similar or slightly larger, since it doesn't scale down
- Everything else (flowers, decor, favors, invitations): genuinely optional, and easiest to cut
  entirely in a micro format without it reading as a compromise

## Where micro weddings create real savings, not just smaller versions

The honest opportunity isn't just "the same wedding, but cheaper" — it's that some categories
(elaborate florals, large-format rentals, big dance floors) stop being necessary at all in a
small, intimate format, rather than just shrinking. That's where the biggest percentage savings
actually come from.
