Budget is where most micro wedding planning either succeeds or quietly falls apart — not because
the numbers are impossible, but because generic wedding budget templates don't reflect how a
small guest list actually changes costs. This guide pulls this site's budget coverage into one
framework.

## Start with what actually changes at a smaller scale

Some categories (catering, rentals, venue capacity) shrink directly with guest count; others
(photography, attire, legal fees) barely move. Understanding which is which prevents both
overspending and unrealistic expectations — the full breakdown is in
[Micro Wedding Budget Breakdown: Real Numbers](/blog/micro-wedding-budget-breakdown).

## If you're closer to eloping than hosting

The numbers shift even further without any guest list at all — photography and travel become the
dominant categories in a way they aren't even in a small guest-inclusive wedding. See
[Elopement Budget Breakdown (What It Actually Costs)](/blog/elopement-budget-breakdown).

## The vendor that deserves the most budget attention

Across both formats, photography is consistently the category people underbudget for relative to
its actual cost and importance — see
[How to Find an Elopement Photographer (Without Overpaying)](/blog/how-to-find-an-elopement-photographer)
for what actually drives that pricing, so you can budget accurately instead of guessing.

## Attire doesn't have to follow traditional budget assumptions

Renting rather than buying is a genuinely practical option for a small, one-day event, and it's
underused relative to how much it can save — see
[Dress Rental Options for Elopements and Micro Weddings](/blog/dress-rental-options-for-elopements-and-micro-weddings).

## A simple allocation approach

Rather than copying a generic wedding budget percentage template, rank your own priorities and
fund them in that order: whichever matters most to you (a specific location, professional
photography, an intimate dinner after) should get first claim on the budget, with everything
else fit around it — not the reverse.

## The mistake that costs people the most

Treating a micro wedding or elopement budget as "a smaller version of a traditional wedding
budget" rather than recognizing it as a genuinely different cost structure is the most common
planning mistake covered across this site's budget guides — the savings come from cutting entire
categories that don't apply at this scale, not just trimming every category a little.
