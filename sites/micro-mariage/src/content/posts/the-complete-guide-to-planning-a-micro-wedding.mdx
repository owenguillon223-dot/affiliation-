Micro wedding planning advice online is scattered across guest-list etiquette, budget breakdowns,
and day-of logistics with little sense of how they connect. This guide ties this site's coverage
together in the order that actually works.

## Start with the guest list — it drives everything else

The guest count determines your budget, your venue size, and your catering needs, so it has to
come first. The structure for making these decisions without unnecessary hurt feelings is in
[How to Cut Your Wedding Guest List Without Drama](/blog/how-to-cut-your-guest-list-without-drama),
and the etiquette questions specific to a small list — plus-ones, kids, coworkers — are covered in
[Micro Wedding Guest List Etiquette: Who to Invite](/blog/micro-wedding-guest-list-etiquette).

## Have the hard conversations early

If cutting the list means disappointing someone close to you, that conversation goes better
before invitations go out than after. See
[How to Tell Family You're Having a Micro Wedding](/blog/how-to-tell-family-you-are-having-a-micro-wedding).

## Build the budget around what actually changes at this scale

A micro wedding's cost structure looks different from a traditional wedding's — some categories
shrink dramatically, others barely change. The full breakdown is in
[Micro Wedding Budget: The Complete Breakdown](/blog/micro-wedding-budget-the-complete-breakdown).

## Set a realistic timeline

Six months is workable for most micro weddings — far shorter than traditional wedding planning
timelines assume. The month-by-month structure is in
[Micro Wedding Checklist: 6 Months to I Do](/blog/micro-wedding-checklist-6-months-to-i-do).

## Handle invitations and attire

Wording that sets expectations clearly, without over-explaining, is covered in
[Micro Wedding Invitation Wording (Templates)](/blog/micro-wedding-invitation-wording). For
attire, renting is worth genuine consideration for a small, one-day event — see
[Dress Rental Options for Elopements and Micro Weddings](/blog/dress-rental-options-for-elopements-and-micro-weddings).

## The order that works, summarized

1. Set the guest list and have any hard conversations early
2. Build the budget around your actual priorities, not a generic wedding budget template
3. Work backward from your date on a compressed but realistic timeline
4. Send invitations that set the tone clearly
5. Handle attire and remaining logistics last — they're the most flexible pieces

If you're weighing whether a micro wedding or a true elopement fits you better before committing
to this path, see
[Micro Wedding vs Elopement: What's the Difference](/blog/micro-wedding-vs-elopement) first.
