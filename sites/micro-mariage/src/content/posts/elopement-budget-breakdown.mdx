> Note: the figures below are general planning guidance, not sourced pricing data — costs vary
> enormously by location, especially for travel and permits. Use this as a framework for your own
> research, not a quote.

Without a guest list at all, an elopement's cost structure looks completely different from even a
micro wedding's. Some categories disappear entirely; a few others become proportionally much
bigger than people expect going in.

## What disappears almost entirely

- Catering for guests (unless a small dinner is planned after)
- Guest-scaled rentals — tables, chairs, linens
- Invitations, in the traditional sense — a simple announcement usually replaces them

## What becomes proportionally bigger

**Photography** is usually the single largest line item in an elopement budget, often by a wide
margin — with no guest list to spend on, photography captures the entire record of the day, and
most couples budget accordingly once they realize this.

**Travel and accommodation**, if eloping somewhere other than home, can rival or exceed every
other cost combined — flights, lodging, and any required travel for witnesses or a photographer
add up fast and are the most commonly underestimated line item in elopement planning.

**Permits**, for many scenic or public locations, are a real and sometimes surprising cost —
research this specifically for your chosen location rather than assuming it's free to get married
somewhere beautiful.

## What stays roughly the same as a traditional wedding

- Marriage license and legal fees
- Rings
- Outfits — though many elopement couples choose simpler attire than they might for a large
  wedding, this isn't a given

## A simple framework for allocating an elopement budget

Rank your priorities honestly before assigning numbers: is this trip-focused (location and
travel matter most), photography-focused (the images matter most), or intimacy-focused (a small
dinner with a few loved ones after matters most)? Whichever is true for you should get the
largest share of the budget — trying to fund all three heavily at once is usually where
elopement budgets balloon past what people expected going in.
