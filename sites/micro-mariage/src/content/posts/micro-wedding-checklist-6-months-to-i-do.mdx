Traditional wedding planning timelines often stretch to 12-18 months, built around vendor
availability for large-scale events and big guest-list logistics. A micro wedding genuinely
doesn't need that much lead time — six months is workable for most couples, and this timeline
reflects what actually needs to happen when.

## 6 months out

- Set the guest list target and budget together, before booking anything (see
  [How to Cut Your Wedding Guest List Without Drama](/blog/how-to-cut-your-guest-list-without-drama))
- Choose a date and secure the venue — even small venues for popular dates can book out this far
  ahead
- Book photography — the vendor most likely to have limited availability, same as with elopements

## 4-5 months out

- Finalize the guest list and collect addresses
- Book any remaining vendors (officiant, caterer if needed, florist if included)
- Choose and order attire, allowing time for alterations

## 2-3 months out

- Send invitations — see
  [Micro Wedding Invitation Wording (Templates)](/blog/micro-wedding-invitation-wording) for how
  to word them clearly for a small guest list
- Confirm final headcount is trending toward your target
- Finalize the day-of schedule and share it with anyone involved in coordinating

## 1 month out

- Confirm final details with every vendor — headcount, timing, any special requests
- Apply for the marriage license, checking your specific location's timing requirements, since
  some have waiting periods or expiration windows that need to align with your date
- Break in your shoes and finalize any last attire details

## Final week

- Confirm final numbers with your caterer or venue
- Pack anything needed for the day, ideally the night before, not the morning of
- Protect unstructured time — resist the urge to schedule every remaining hour before the wedding

## What compresses well vs. what doesn't

Guest-dependent logistics (catering counts, seating, rentals) compress fastest with a smaller
guest list. Vendor booking timelines (especially photography) compress the least — popular
photographers and venues book out on their own schedule regardless of your wedding's size.
