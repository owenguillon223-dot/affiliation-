"Micro wedding" and "elopement" get used almost interchangeably in casual conversation, but
they're different enough in practice that conflating them leads to mismatched planning advice —
budgets, timelines, and vendor needs differ meaningfully between the two.

## The core difference: guests

A **micro wedding** is a small but still guest-inclusive event — typically somewhere in the range
of 10-50 people, with most of the traditional wedding structure (ceremony, some form of
reception) just scaled down.

An **elopement** traditionally means just the couple, sometimes with an officiant and
photographer present and little else — though the term has broadened in recent years to include
"elopements" with a handful of witnesses, which blurs the line with a very small micro wedding.

## Why the distinction matters for planning

- **Budget structure differs** — a micro wedding still has per-guest costs (catering, rentals)
  that a true elopement doesn't, while an elopement often puts a much larger share of the budget
  into photography and travel
- **Legal logistics differ slightly** — some locations have witness requirements for a legal
  marriage that affect a true elopement more directly than a guest-inclusive micro wedding
- **Timeline needs differ** — an elopement can often be planned faster since there's no guest
  coordination involved at all

## If you're not sure which fits you

Ask what you actually want the day to include: if any celebration with loved ones present
matters to you, even a small one, you're likely planning a micro wedding, not an elopement. If
the appeal is genuinely just the two of you, with celebrating others coming separately (or not
at all), that's an elopement.

## Hybrid approaches are common

followed by a separate, larger celebration later — getting some of the intimacy of an elopement
