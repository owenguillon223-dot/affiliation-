An elopement day has far fewer moving parts than a traditional wedding, but that doesn't mean it
needs no plan at all — a loose structure prevents the two most common elopement-day problems:
rushing the ceremony to make a sunset photo window, or running out of daylight entirely.

## A sample structure (adjust freely)

**Morning: getting ready**
Build in more time than feels necessary — nerves and unfamiliar locations both tend to slow
things down more than expected.

**Midday: travel to location**
If your ceremony spot requires a hike or drive, pad this segment generously. Arriving early and
having a few unhurried minutes beats arriving rushed.

**Ceremony**
Keep this segment's timing flexible rather than fixed to the minute — weather, light, or simply
wanting a few extra minutes are all common and worth allowing for.

**Photos**
This is usually the longest block of the day by far, and it's worth protecting — most elopement
photographers structure the whole day timeline around getting the best light, which often means
building the ceremony time around photography needs, not the other way around.

**Golden hour, if included**
Many elopement photographers prioritize the hour before sunset for a portion of the shoot — if
this matters to you, confirm your ceremony timing works backward from that window rather than
hoping it lines up.

**Evening: dinner or celebration**
Whether it's just the two of you or a small group, build in unstructured time here rather than
scheduling it tightly — after a day that's often more physically and emotionally full than
expected, flexibility matters more than a precise schedule.

## The one thing worth over-planning

Confirm sunset time and golden hour timing for your specific date and location in advance — this
is the one fixed constraint in an otherwise flexible day, and it's worth working the rest of the
schedule backward from it rather than treating it as an afterthought.

## Build in a buffer, not a tight schedule

Unlike a traditional wedding with vendors and guests to coordinate, an elopement's biggest
scheduling risk is usually just you and your partner running behind — build 30-60 minutes of
slack into the day rather than planning back-to-back with no room for delay.
