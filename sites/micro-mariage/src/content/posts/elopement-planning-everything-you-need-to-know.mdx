Elopement planning content is often either purely inspirational or purely legal, rarely
sequenced together in a way that's actually usable. This guide connects this site's full
elopement coverage in planning order.

## First, understand what you're actually planning

"Elopement" gets used loosely — make sure the format matches what you actually want before
diving into planning specifics. See
[Micro Wedding vs Elopement: What's the Difference](/blog/micro-wedding-vs-elopement) if you're
not sure yet.

## The full step-by-step process

The complete sequence — legal requirements, location, budget, vendors, and finally the day
itself — is in
[How to Plan an Elopement Step by Step](/blog/how-to-plan-an-elopement-step-by-step). This is the
central guide everything else on this site connects back to.

## Choosing where

Rather than chasing a generic "best destinations" ranking, choose based on landscape type,
permit requirements, and what actually matters to you — covered in
[Best Elopement Destinations: How to Actually Choose One](/blog/best-elopement-destinations).

## Budgeting realistically

An elopement's cost structure is genuinely different from a traditional wedding's — photography
and travel dominate in a way catering and rentals do for a bigger event. The full breakdown is in
[Elopement Budget Breakdown (What It Actually Costs)](/blog/elopement-budget-breakdown).

## Booking the vendor that matters most

Photography is usually the highest-cost, highest-stakes elopement booking, and often the first
one to lock in given how far in advance popular photographers book. See
[How to Find an Elopement Photographer (Without Overpaying)](/blog/how-to-find-an-elopement-photographer).

## Planning the day itself

Once everything else is set, build a flexible day-of structure rather than a rigid schedule — see
[Elopement Day Timeline (Free Printable)](/blog/elopement-day-timeline).

## The order that works, summarized

1. Confirm you're planning the right format (elopement vs. micro wedding)
2. Check legal requirements for your location before anything else
3. Choose a location based on real priorities, not just photos
4. Set a realistic budget, expecting photography and travel to dominate
5. Book your photographer early
6. Plan the day itself last — it's the most flexible piece
