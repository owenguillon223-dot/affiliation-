Micro wedding invitations have a job traditional invitations don't: they need to set expectations
about scale without reading as an explanation or apology. The wording matters more here than on a
standard invitation.

## The core principle: state it plainly, once

The most common mistake is over-explaining — a paragraph justifying the small guest list.
One clear, warm line does more work than a defensive explanation, and it sets the tone for
everyone reading it.

## Template: close friends and family only

> [Names] are getting married, and we're keeping it small — surrounded by the people who mean
> the most to us. We'd love for you to be part of it.

## Template: when the couple is eloping and announcing after

> We said "I do" with just the two of us on [date] in [location]. We wanted to share the news
> with the people we love, and we can't wait to celebrate together soon.

## Template: for a micro wedding with a larger celebration to follow

> Our wedding day will be small and intimate, just close family. We'd love for you to join us
> for a celebration afterward on [date] — details to follow.

This wording matters especially if you're navigating hurt feelings from people not on the small
guest list — see
[How to Cut Your Wedding Guest List Without Drama](/blog/how-to-cut-your-guest-list-without-drama)
and
[How to Tell Family You're Having a Micro Wedding](/blog/how-to-tell-family-you-are-having-a-micro-wedding)
for the conversations that should happen before invitations go out, not because of them.

## What to include beyond the core wording

- Clear date, time, and location, same as any invitation
- RSVP details, ideally with a firm deadline given the smaller, more predictable logistics of a
  micro event
- Any dress code or specific guidance relevant to an intimate setting (many micro weddings and
  elopement-adjacent celebrations skip formal dress codes entirely)

## Keep the design simple to match the tone

Invitation wording that emphasizes intimacy reads oddly paired with an elaborate, formal design —
simpler stationery generally matches a micro wedding's tone better than the ornate templates
designed for traditional-scale weddings.
