Elopement planning content is often either purely inspirational (photo galleries, mood boards)
or purely legal (license requirements), rarely both in the order you actually need them. Here's
the sequence that avoids the most common mistakes.

## Step 1: confirm the legal requirements first

Before booking anything, confirm marriage license requirements for wherever you're eloping —
waiting periods, residency requirements, and document needs vary significantly by location and
can derail a date if discovered late. This is the step most likely to cause a real problem if
skipped, and it's the cheapest to check early.

## Step 2: pick a location that matches your actual priorities

Decide what matters most before browsing photos — a specific landscape, proximity to family for
after, ease of travel, or budget. Elopement locations get chosen on aesthetics alone more often
than any other wedding decision, which is how people end up somewhere logistically painful for
a day that's supposed to be simple.

## Step 3: set a realistic budget before booking anyone

Even a small elopement has real costs — photographer, officiant, possibly a permit fee for the
location, travel. See
[Elopement Budget Breakdown (What It Actually Costs)](/blog/elopement-budget-breakdown) before
committing to a location that assumes a bigger budget than you actually have.

## Step 4: book the photographer early, everything else after

Photographers for popular elopement dates and locations book out further ahead than almost any
other vendor — this is usually the first booking to lock in, not the last. See
[How to Find an Elopement Photographer (Without Overpaying)](/blog/how-to-find-an-elopement-photographer).

## Step 5: handle permits and location-specific requirements

Many scenic or public elopement locations require a permit, especially for photography — check
this well before the date, since some permits have their own lead time and application windows.

## Step 6: plan the actual day, last

Once the legal, financial, and vendor pieces are locked, plan the sequence of the day itself —
see [Elopement Day Timeline (Free Printable)](/blog/elopement-day-timeline). This step comes
last on purpose: it's the most flexible and least likely to cause a problem if it changes.

## Realistic timeline

A straightforward elopement can genuinely be planned in as little as a few weeks if legal
requirements allow it — the biggest variable is usually photographer availability, not anything
else on this list. Don't assume you need the same lead time as a traditional wedding; you almost
certainly don't.
