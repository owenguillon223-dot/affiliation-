Every micro wedding conversation eventually runs into the same wall: not the venue, not the
budget, but the guest list. Cutting a list from 150 to 30 means telling people no, and that's the
part most planning advice skips past.

## Start with a number, not a list

Pick your target guest count before you write a single name down. Working the other direction —
writing everyone you'd like to invite, then cutting — anchors you emotionally to a bigger list
and makes every cut feel like a loss instead of a decision made from the start.

## Use tiers, not a single yes/no pass

Sort names into three tiers instead of trying to make one hard decision per person:

- **Tier 1: non-negotiable** — immediate family, closest friends
- **Tier 2: would love if the number allows** — the people you'd invite at 50 but not at 20
- **Tier 3: obligation invites** — people you'd invite mainly out of social pressure, not desire

This makes the actual cutting mechanical: fill Tier 1 first, add Tier 2 until you hit your
number, and treat Tier 3 as the first names to reconsider, not the last.

## The "would they invite me" test isn't reliable

A common piece of guest-list advice is to only invite people who'd invite you to their own
wedding — but this assumes symmetry that doesn't always exist (age gaps, life stage, distance).
Use it as one data point, not the deciding rule.

## Apply the cut evenly across "sides"

Uneven cuts — one family's guest list barely touched while another's is slashed — cause more
hurt feelings than the total number does. Agree on a rough even split before finalizing names,
even if the exact ratio isn't perfectly 50/50.

## Have the hard conversations early, not after invitations go out

If you know a cut will be painful for someone close to you, a direct conversation before
invitations are sent goes over far better than someone finding out by absence. See
[How to Tell Family You're Having a Micro Wedding](/blog/how-to-tell-family-you-are-having-a-micro-wedding)
for how to frame that conversation.

## Remember what the smaller list buys you

A tighter guest list isn't just a budget lever — it's usually the single biggest factor in
whether a wedding day feels intimate and manageable rather than logistically overwhelming. Keep
that trade-off in view when a cut feels hard; it's not just subtraction, it's what makes the
smaller format work in the first place.
