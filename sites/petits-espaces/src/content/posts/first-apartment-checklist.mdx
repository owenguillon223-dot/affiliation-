Most first-apartment checklists are just shopping lists — furniture, kitchenware, cleaning
supplies. Those matter, but the things that actually cause problems in the first weeks of living
alone are usually administrative, not decorative: utilities not set up, a lease detail missed,
no plan for how bills get paid.

## Before move-in day

- **Document the apartment's condition** — photos and video of every room, every wall, every
  appliance, timestamped, before you move a single box in. This is what protects your security
  deposit later, and it's the single most skipped step on this list.
- **Set up utilities to start on move-in day**, not after — electricity, gas, and internet often
  take longer to activate than people expect, especially internet installation appointments.
- **Confirm what's actually included** — some rentals include appliances or furnishings that
  others don't; know exactly what you're supplying yourself before shopping for anything.

## The first week

- **Locate the water shutoff, breaker panel, and fire extinguisher** — not urgent until suddenly
  very urgent; find them in week one, not during an emergency.
- **Set up mail forwarding and update your address** on the accounts that actually matter first
  (bank, employer, ID) rather than all at once.
- **Test smoke detectors** and replace batteries if needed — landlords vary widely in how
  recently these were checked before you moved in.

## Setting up the space itself

Don't try to fully furnish and decorate in the first week — prioritize the essentials that make
the space livable (bed, basic kitchen setup, a place to sit) and let the rest happen over the
first month. A first apartment that's 70% set up and lived-in beats one where you're still living
out of boxes trying to get it "finished" first.

## Ongoing, not one-time

A few things are easy to forget because they don't come up until later: know your lease's notice
period for moving out, know who to call for maintenance requests, and keep a copy of your signed
lease somewhere you can actually find it. None of these matter in week one — all of them matter
eventually.
