Moving into a first apartment involves administrative tasks, physical setup, and — if you're
sharing the space — coordination with another person, and most advice covers only one of those
three at a time. This guide ties them together in the order they actually happen.

## Before and during the move

Timing is what most moving advice gets wrong — booking movers, transferring utilities, and
packing all have different ideal lead times before move-in day. The full week-by-week breakdown
is in
[Moving Checklist Printable + Timeline](/blog/moving-checklist-printable-and-timeline).

## The first two weeks in the space

Documentation, utilities, and safety basics matter more in the first weeks than decorating does
— covered in full in
[First Apartment Checklist (Free Printable)](/blog/first-apartment-checklist). Photograph the
apartment's condition before you unpack a single box; it's the step people skip most and need
most if a deposit dispute ever comes up.

## Getting the physical space organized

Once the administrative basics are handled, the actual organizing work follows the same
zone-first approach covered in
[The Complete Small Apartment Organization Guide](/blog/the-complete-small-apartment-organization-guide)
— don't rush this. A space that's genuinely settled after a month beats one that looks finished
after a chaotic weekend and then stays cluttered for months.

## If you're sharing the apartment

Chore division is the single most common source of roommate conflict, and it's almost entirely
preventable with a written, revisited agreement — see
[Roommate Chore Chart Printable](/blog/roommate-chore-chart-printable) for a structure that holds
up past the first month.

## Keeping it that way

A first deep clean, done properly, resets the space and is a natural moment to also reset any
zone dividers or organization systems that have drifted since move-in — see
[How to Deep Clean a Small Apartment (Room by Room Printable)](/blog/how-to-deep-clean-a-small-apartment).

## The order that works

1. Plan and execute the move on a realistic timeline
2. Handle the administrative first-two-weeks basics
3. Organize the space zone by zone, not all at once
4. If sharing, agree on chores in writing early
5. Maintain with a periodic deep clean and reset, not a one-time effort
