A small rental kitchen usually has two compounding problems: limited cabinet space, and no
ability to change the cabinets themselves. Most of the real gains come from using cabinet
*volume* better, not from adding more surfaces.

## Stack vertically inside cabinets

Cabinet shelves are almost always spaced for the tallest item you might store, which wastes
height for everything shorter. A stackable shelf riser inside a cabinet turns one shelf's worth
of vertical space into two usable layers — one of the highest-value, lowest-effort upgrades
available in a small kitchen.

## The inside of cabinet doors is free real estate

Door-mounted racks (for spices, foil and wrap boxes, or cutting boards) use space that's
otherwise completely wasted. This is genuinely free capacity — nothing else in the kitchen
competes for the inside of a cabinet door.

## Drawer dividers stop drawers from becoming junk piles

A utensil drawer without dividers reverts to chaos within a week, no matter how well you organize
it initially. Adjustable dividers (not custom-cut, just tension-fit) keep categories separated
without any permanent modification to the drawer itself.

## Use vertical wall space above counters, carefully

A tension-mounted rail system (not drilled) above a counter can hold hanging utensils, small
pots, or a spice rack, freeing counter and cabinet space for things that can't hang. Check that
whatever you mount stays clear of any stove exhaust or heat source — placement matters as much as
the mounting method here.

## Rethink what "pantry" means in a small kitchen

Without a dedicated pantry, over-the-door or freestanding narrow shelving units near (not in) the
kitchen can absorb overflow pantry items, keeping actual kitchen cabinets reserved for daily-use
items. A narrow rolling cart that fits in a gap between the fridge and a wall is a commonly
underused source of extra storage in small kitchens specifically.

## Match storage method to what's actually crowding the kitchen

Before buying anything, notice what's actually causing the clutter — is it too many dishes, not
enough counter space, or overflow pantry goods? The fixes above solve different problems, and
buying storage without identifying which one applies is the most common way small-kitchen
organizing purchases go unused.
