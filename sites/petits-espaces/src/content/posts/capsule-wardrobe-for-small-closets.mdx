Every closet organization trick has a ceiling — there's only so much a shelf divider or a second
rod can do if the closet is simply holding more clothes than it was built for. A capsule
wardrobe attacks the actual constraint instead of working around it.

## Start by auditing, not purging

Before removing anything, spend a week noting what you actually wear. Most people wear a small
fraction of their wardrobe in regular rotation — seeing that in writing, rather than guessing,
makes the next step far less overwhelming and far more accurate.

## Build around a limited color palette

A wardrobe where most pieces coordinate with most other pieces multiplies your outfit
combinations without adding more items — this is the actual mechanism behind a capsule wardrobe
working, not just "owning fewer things." A tight color palette is what makes a small item count
feel complete rather than limited.

## Separate "capsule" from "everything you own"

A capsule wardrobe doesn't mean getting rid of formal wear you need twice a year or seasonal
items — those can live in under-bed or overflow storage (see
[Best Under-Bed Storage for Small Bedrooms](/blog/best-under-bed-storage-for-small-bedrooms)) while
the closet itself holds only your current-season, regularly-worn rotation.

## A reasonable starting number

There's no single correct capsule size, but a commonly cited starting range is 30-40 pieces
(including basics) for a season — treat that as a rough anchor to adjust from based on your own
life and climate, not a strict rule to hit exactly.

## What to do with what you remove

Sell, donate, or store seasonally rather than defaulting to storage for everything you're
uncertain about — genuine removal is what creates the closet-space benefit; moving the same
volume of clothes to a different storage spot just relocates the problem.

## Maintaining it

The habit that actually sustains a capsule wardrobe is a one-in-one-out rule for new purchases,
not a periodic big cleanout. Reapply the same audit-before-purging approach every season rather
than letting the wardrobe slowly regrow to its old size.

Once your wardrobe fits the space, the closet techniques in
[Small Closet Organization Without a Closet System](/blog/small-closet-organization-without-a-closet-system)
go much further with less volume to manage.
