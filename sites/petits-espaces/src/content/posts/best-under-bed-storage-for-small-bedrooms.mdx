Under-bed space is often the single largest block of unused storage in a small bedroom, and it's
routinely wasted because whatever gets shoved under there becomes impossible to reach without
pulling everything out.

## Match the container to your bed's actual clearance

Measure the clearance under your bed frame before buying anything — this sounds obvious but is
the most common mistake, since bed frame heights vary enormously and a container that doesn't
fit is useless no matter how well designed it is. Low-profile bins are made specifically for
platform beds and other low-clearance frames.

## Rolling containers beat static ones for anything you access often

If you're pulling something out from under the bed more than occasionally, a container on wheels
or a slide-out drawer system is worth the extra cost — reaching under a bed to drag out a static
bin is the exact friction that causes under-bed storage to get abandoned and ignored.

## What belongs under a bed (and what doesn't)

**Good fits:** off-season clothing, spare bedding, shoes you wear occasionally, keepsakes you
don't need frequent access to.

**Poor fits:** anything you need daily, anything that can't handle a bit of dust exposure over
time, and anything heavy enough that pulling it out becomes a chore in itself.

## Vacuum-seal bags for soft goods, not everything

Vacuum-seal bags dramatically shrink the footprint of clothing, bedding, and other soft items,
which matters most under a low-clearance bed frame. They're a poor fit for anything with rigid
shape (shoes, structured bags) since compressing them can damage the item.

## Keep a simple inventory if you use this space heavily

Once storage is out of sight, it's easy to forget what's actually under there — a short list
taped inside a closet door of what's stored where prevents the common problem of buying a
duplicate of something you already own but forgot about.
